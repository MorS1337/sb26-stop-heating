\section{Benchmark integrity}
\label{sec:integrity}

The reward is computed from a simulator whose cost accounting is not closed under the
policy's action space. Two seams are public and both have a proposed fix: the hot-water
tank is billed from its setpoint rather than from its burner \citep{stncsn2026pr2}, and
the return air is mixed by floor area rather than by airflow \citep{stncsn2026pr3}.
Neither fix is merged and the leaderboard runs the unfixed tree, so what each seam is
worth---to whom, and whether it survives correction---is a measurement, taken at the model
calls by an instrument that leaves every per-step reward bit-identical and reproduces the
published return-air fix exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:integrity}). % src: docs/integrity_audit.md §2.3-2.4
% src: the Kaggle forum digest (not released) B1/B2; docs/integrity_audit.md §2

\emph{B1 is a gift an honest controller does not take.} A probe applying the published
setpoint recipe on top of CtrlW delivers the same $26.72$\,MWh$_{\text{th}}$ of reheat
while $11.1$\,MWh of gas stops being billed, comfort does not move ($-0.539$ against
$-0.541$), and the anchor improves by $+36.93$ units ($+39.7\,\%$)---$3.3$ times the gap
to the best public score on the same episode. It is pure accounting: on the corrected tree
the same probe is $0.59$ units \emph{worse} than doing nothing special, because a tank
that actually cools delivers colder water. Across dev30, gated to the base controller's own
heating periods, the recipe takes CtrlW5-278 from 0.0071980 to 0.0058864 ($-18.2\,\%$; $-36\,\%$
on Fairbanks, $0$ in Singapore) and costs $0.46\,\%$ corrected: a fifth of an honest score is
free to whoever cycles the tank.

An independent residual-PPO entry found the same
seam without being told of it: over 30 Fairbanks days its policy drew 914\,kWh more tank heat
than it was billed for, $39\,\%$ of its gain \citep{alex2026residualppo}.

\emph{B2 is worth nothing to a controller that moves air well.} The manufactured heat is
$0.7$--$1.0\,\%$ of the coil heat the three well-scoring controllers buy, and the artifact is worth
\emph{minus} a fraction of a unit ($-0.34$, $-0.42$, $-0.86$; Table~\ref{tab:integrity}).
Harvesting it is priced out by the fan and by comfort: the trade clears only above
$\Delta T\approx P/282$\,K against the $0.12$--$0.59$\,K the field supplies. All
three controllers improve when the accounting is fixed, and more so on a mild scenario
($10.4\,\%$ on a full-length Atlanta episode), while the probe reverses there: B1's worth is
scenario-dependent and the anchor figure is a ceiling.
% src: docs/integrity_audit.md §3.1-3.2, §4-6

The third public channel is a leak rather than a seam: the occupancy generator uses one seed
everywhere and the seed is not exposed, so replaying it yields the per-zone schedule, worth the
foresight number of \S\ref{sec:voi} ($2.79\,\%$). Final verification reseeds, so we never use it.
% src: docs/voi_oracle.md §9; the Kaggle forum digest (not released) B7

\paragraph{The anchor gap was a control question, and the answer is sequencing.} On the
seven-day anchor CtrlW scores $-93.12$ against $-81.9$ for a physically consistent public
controller \citep{stncsn2026pr2}, $-85.4$ for a twin-plus-LP planner and $-79.1$ for a
free-action LP bound on the linearised building \citep{alayet2026anchor} (a bound, not a
controller). Neither information
(the strongest channel, perfect per-zone foresight, is worth $2.60$ units against the
$11.22$-unit gap to $-81.9$, so $4.3$ times too little), nor B2 (a fraction of a unit), nor B1 ($-0.3$ units for the PR's own
controller, $-81.9\to-81.6$, as its author reports) explains it, and an 8\,h receding-horizon
planner moves $7.6\,\%$ the wrong way. What explains it is \S\ref{sec:ctrlw5}: the family heats
the empty building at night. Releasing the floor takes the same controller to $-80.31$---1.6
units better than the best public number we know of, though worse than the free-action bound,
which is an optimum and not an entry---with the frost-limited version we ship at $-86.68$. We
therefore report the gap as \emph{closed by scheduling for the controllers we can compute this
on}, and attribute nothing to other teams beyond their published numbers: the audit bounds what
these seams can be worth to a controller that respects comfort, not what any particular team
did.
