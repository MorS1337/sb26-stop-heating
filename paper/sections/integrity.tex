\section{Benchmark integrity}
\label{sec:integrity}

The reward is computed from a simulator whose cost accounting is not closed under the
policy's action space. Two seams are public and both have a proposed fix: the hot-water
tank is billed from its setpoint rather than from its burner (B1, \citep{stncsn2026pr2}), and
the return air is mixed by floor area rather than by airflow (B2, \citep{stncsn2026pr3}).
Neither fix is merged and the leaderboard runs the unfixed tree, so what each seam is
worth is a measurement, taken by an instrument that leaves every per-step reward bit-identical
(Appendix~\ref{app:integrity}). % src: docs/integrity_audit.md §2.3-2.4
% src: the Kaggle forum digest (not released) B1/B2; docs/integrity_audit.md §2

\emph{B1 rewards an exploit.} A probe applying the published
setpoint recipe on top of CtrlW delivers the same $26.72$\,MWh$_{\text{th}}$ of reheat
while $11.1$\,MWh of gas stops being billed, comfort does not move, and the anchor improves
by $+36.93$ units over CtrlW ($+39.7\,\%$), $3.3$ times the gap to the best public score there. It is pure
accounting: on the corrected tree the same probe is $0.59$ units \emph{worse}. Across dev30,
gated to the base controller's own heating periods, the recipe takes CtrlW5-278 from 0.0071980
to 0.0058864 ($-18.2\,\%$; $-36\,\%$ on Fairbanks, $0$ in Singapore) and costs $0.46\,\%$
corrected: a fifth of an honest score is free to whoever cycles the tank.

A learned policy finds the seam untold: $39\,\%$ of a residual-PPO entry's gain was unbilled
tank heat \citep{alex2026residualppo}.
% src: the Kaggle forum update (not released) §4 (alexchilton notebook §4b, 2026-11-06)

\emph{B2 hardly matters to a controller that moves air well.} The manufactured heat is
$0.7$--$1.0\,\%$ of the coil heat the three well-scoring controllers buy, and the artifact is worth
\emph{minus} a fraction of a unit ($-0.34$, $-0.42$, $-0.86$; Table~\ref{tab:integrity}):
harvesting it is priced out by the fan and by comfort. All three controllers improve when the
accounting is fixed, more so on a mild scenario ($10.4\,\%$ on a full-length Atlanta episode),
where the B1 probe reverses: its worth is scenario-dependent and the anchor figure is a ceiling.
% src: docs/integrity_audit.md §3.1-3.2, §4-6
The third public channel is a leak: the occupancy generator runs on the kit's
one default seed, which the observation does not expose, so replaying it would yield the per-zone
schedule, whose value is the foresight number of \S\ref{sec:voi} ($2.79\,\%$). Final verification reseeds, so we never use it.
% src: docs/voi_oracle.md §9; the Kaggle forum digest (not released) B7

\paragraph{B1 also overcharges an honest controller.} When the base controller idles the loop
after two hours without demand, the
shipped tank falls to 310\,K unbilled, and the first call of the next morning is billed for
the recharge: in a mild seven-day week CtrlX is billed $1.055$ units of gas per unit of heat its
coils draw ($1.017$ in a cold one; $1.099$ before the off-peak tank floor), and its shipped dev30
score is $1.9\,\%$ above its corrected one. The cleanest measurement is a change that does nothing physically. The
burner raises the tank by at most $2.5$\,K per step in every scenario, so a setpoint capped at
the observed tank temperature plus $2.5$\,K commands the same trajectory: on the corrected tree
all ten benchmark scores are identical to the last digit, and on the shipped tree the capped
controller scores $0.55\,\%$ better. Holding the tank setpoint through the idle branch and at its
30-hour peak, under that cap, removes the overcharge without ever billing less than the coils
draw ($1.035$ and $1.004$): the shipped score then meets the corrected one
(Table~\ref{tab:main}) and the leaderboard entry moves from 0.01002 to 0.00982 (58217787). We
report that variant apart, because it is a response to the accounting: it costs
$0.05\,\%$ on the corrected tree and is worth nothing once the fixes land. A
corollary for reading the public board: the late start of \S\ref{sec:ctrlx} is worth $0.33\,\%$
on the corrected development set and nothing on the leaderboard ($0.01007\to0.01008$), because
the unfixed billing charges the later morning call. We attribute nothing to other teams beyond
their published numbers: the audit bounds what these seams can be worth to a controller that
respects comfort; it says nothing about what any particular team did.
