Every claim in the paper compares controllers on the same trajectory, so these studies are only as good
as the number of episodes they can afford, and a speed-up that flips a damper decision is a
different experiment. The whole of \texttt{env.step} now runs as one C call that reproduces the
simulator's order-dependent behaviours: twelve closed-loop episodes (three buildings, both
trees, two occupancy seeds) and full 8\,641-step traces are bit-identical in observations and
rewards, at $2.1$, $1.8$ and $1.6\times$ on the 30-, 79- and 85-zone buildings; five pure-Python
patches recover $1.6$--$1.9\times$ where C is unavailable. The shipped SuperLU is already optimal
for a single right-hand side, batching is worth $4.1$--$4.9\times$, a correct GPU solver loses to
one CPU core, and a $200$-mode reduced model drifts $1.0$--$1.2$\,K yet reproduces a five-way
policy ranking exactly---a shortlisting filter, not a simulator (Table~\ref{tab:tools},
Appendix~\ref{app:tools}).
