\section{Tools: an exact fast simulator}
\label{sec:tools}

Every claim above compares controllers on the same trajectory, so these studies are only as good
as the number of episodes they can afford: one step costs $28.1$\,ms of CPU on the smallest
building (thermal solve $13.7$, rest of the HVAC step $6.0$, reward and observation assembly
$8.4$, plus the policy's $1.8$ outside \texttt{env.step}), against the $\approx\!125$\,ms per step
the 9-hour rule allows. Both halves must fall, and neither may change
the trajectory: a speed-up that flips a damper decision is a different experiment.
% src: docs/fastsim_rom.md §12

The whole of \texttt{env.step}---zone means, thermostat decisions, the damper floor,
supply-air temperature, boiler and hot-water demand, gains, the finite-difference solve with
its Woodbury air side, the air-side fold-back, convection, the observation vector and the
reward numbers---now runs as one C call, with the policy-facing half ($\approx0.4$\,ms) and
the array API ($\approx0.2$\,ms) left in Python. % src: cenv/fused.py docstring
Two simulator behaviours dictated the design (the boiler's sensor mutates the tank on every read,
and zone thermal power is assigned rather than accumulated), and the kernel reproduces both.
The gate is twelve closed-loop episodes (three buildings, both trees, two occupancy seeds) with
observations and rewards bit-identical in all twelve ($\max|\Delta r_t|=0.0$; zone temperatures
$\le1.2\e{15}$ relative, i.e.\ the policy-visible quantities are bit-identical, not the
field), plus full 8\,641-step traces on both trees identical to the last bit.
Cost per step falls from $20.5$ to $9.6$\,ms on the 30-zone building ($2.1\times$),
$49.8\to27.8$ ($1.8\times$) on the 79-zone one and $67.6\to42.0$ ($1.6\times$) on the 85-zone one; five pure-Python
patches recover $1.6$--$1.9\times$ where C is unavailable. % src: cenv/fused.py "Gate", "Measured"; docs/fast_exact.md
The shipped SuperLU is already optimal for a single right-hand side, batching is worth
$4.1$--$4.9\times$, a correct GPU solver loses to one CPU core, and a $200$-mode reduced model
drifts $1.0$--$1.2$\,K yet reproduces a five-way policy ranking exactly---a shortlisting filter,
not a simulator (Table~\ref{tab:tools}, Appendix~\ref{app:tools}).
