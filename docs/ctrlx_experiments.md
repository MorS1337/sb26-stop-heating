## Rules (non-negotiable)

**Fair play**
- Policy modules use numpy + the standard library only. No building, weather or station identifiers. The harness enforces this.
- No zone below +4 °C at any step (harness-enforced). The 278 K standby frost floor is a product decision, not a tuning knob.
- Do not use simulator artifacts:
  - **B1**, the shipped tree's boiler/tank billing seam: tank heat disappears unbilled when the setpoint drops;
  - **B2**, the return-air / mixing-box seam: free heat or cooling from moving lots of air;
  - **B7**, the occupancy RNG seed.
  - See `docs/integrity_audit.md`. The benchmark runs the *corrected* tree with a fixed seed for exactly this reason.
- Gains that exist only on the shipped tree are seam gains. Example: the "idle tank hold" (shipped +1.3..5%, corrected −0.2%). Such gains are reported separately and kept out of the honest controller.
- Policy cost must stay well under ~5 ms/step (HEAD: ~1.4 ms).

**Scope**
- Edit `policies/ctrl_x.py` and new sibling modules in `policies/`.
- Never modify `policies/ctrl_w*.py`, `policies/wu_core.py`, `policies/wu_weights.py`, `autoresearch.sh`, `sbx/arbench.py`, `sbx/evalrun.py`, `sims/`, `starter-kit/`, `cenv/`, `results/`, `paper/`. They hold the paper's controllers, the measurement harness and the simulators.

**Kaggle**
- CPU kernels only, never GPU/TPU (`kaggle/push.sh` refuses).
- At most 4 concurrent kernels.
- At most 5 submissions per UTC day.
- **Never submit without full validation** (below).

## How to measure

**Benchmark (the optimisation target)**
- `bash autoresearch.sh` runs `sbx/arbench.py`: 10 fixed scenarios × 7 days (2016 steps), corrected tree, occupancy seed 12345, exact C step, 2 workers.
- It takes ~3 min on the Mac and is deterministic: reruns are bit-identical.
- Output:
  - `METRIC score=…`, the mean over scenarios of −(mean per-step reward), plus per-station scores, `comfort_e4`, `energy_e3`, `carbon_e3`, `min_zone_C`, `policy_ms`;
  - one `[arbench] <scenario> <score>` line per scenario.
- It exits non-zero on a failed scenario, a frost violation or a rule violation.
- **fairbanks_1 is ~48% of the benchmark mean** (0.0418 of the 10-scenario sum 0.0876). Look at the per-scenario split, not just the mean.
- The benchmark has no flag for parameters. To test a PARAMS variant, edit `CtrlX.PARAMS`, run, then `git checkout policies/ctrl_x.py`. Never edit `ctrl_x.py` while a benchmark runs: every scenario process re-imports it.

## What is known about the problem (measured)

**Where the score comes from**
- Comfort is ~0.7% of the score (benchmark: `comfort_e4` 0.61 vs `energy_e3` 4.14 + `carbon_e3` 4.55). Most of it is at 06–08h, the first arrivals.
- Margin, lead and lift changes trade comfort against energy almost one for one near HEAD. Always compare both components by hour (`by_hour` in the evalrun JSON).
- Heating weeks: gas is 95–98% of plant power. denver_2 (7 days): gas 94.8%, blower 5.2%, pump 0.17%. fairbanks_1 (30 days): gas 98.4%, blower 1.25%, pump 0.34%.
- Wu sets the pump at dp = 1.05·max(need)², a 5% pump headroom. Removing it would save ≤ 0.03% (not done).

**Thermal picture (2026-11-09, `scratch/arbench/probe_fb1.py`, `trace_x2.py`)**
- The zone air is light: ~3 kW moves it ~2.5 K in one step (≈180 kJ/K), and with the plant off it relaxes to the fabric within a step (the first off-step drops a floor-held zone 1.5 K). The fabric (slab, walls) is slow: ~0.07 K/step at −35 °C, and holding the air 4 K higher for 12 steps warmed it only ~0.7 K.
- So a "zone temperature" is a quasi-static balance of the fabric, the walls, the supply air and the reheat. Wu's one-step model with a frozen gain and a residual EMA integrator still works because the residual learns the pinning (−3 K/step for a zone held 1.8 K above its fabric), but it is clipped at ±5 K/step (mechanism 11) and it converges over tens of steps.
- Frost-floor hold at −37 °C (fb1 Saturday, 32 of 418 units/week): 30 of 39 zones sit on the floor, the plant runs 100% of the time at 111 kW, 43% of which is the 30% outside air at the minimum flow. The coldest zones' passive equilibrium is ~2 °C, 3 K below the floor, so the hold cannot be duty-cycled: any off-step violates the floor. Four duty-cycle designs (below) all lost.
- Morning in a mild week (denver_2): the plant is off 19:00–04:00 and all weekend; 57% of the 04–10h gas is outside air; the 03–06h window is 27–28% of the denver_2/atlanta_8 bills. In deep cold (fb1) charging the fabric at a low air temperature is cheap per watt (gas per net watt 1 + 0.3·(T_zone−T_out)/(T_dis−T_zone): 1.6 at 285 K vs 2.2 at 294 K), which is why fb1 keeps the ramp (mechanism 10's gate).
- Steady heating at 294 K costs 2.16 W of gas per net watt (fb1 midday: 182 of 323 kW is outside air). The ratio is flow-independent at the discharge cap, so recovery speed, pressure and damper choices cannot change it; only the hours the plant runs and the air temperature while it runs can.
- The reward's comfort weight is occupancy-normalised: a 1 K shortfall in one zone for one step costs 0.12/Z in expectation (Z = zones), whatever the headcount; a uniform 1 K shortfall costs 0.12/step at any headcount. Against ~2.8e-4 per kW-step of gas, holding one fb1 zone 1 K warmer (≈1.7 kW net × 2.16) is worth it 3:1 while the zone is occupied.

**What is fixed or unknowable**
- The reward's comfort setpoints equal the comfort window, 294/297 K.
- The plant sits on its device floors: OA 30%, damper 0.1, static pressure 200 Pa, discharge 308.15 K.
- Wu's zone model `theta` is frozen. `_learn` only updates a residual EMA. `residual_alpha` 0.75 is near-optimal: 0 gives +0.79%, 1 gives +2.78%. `disturbance_rate` 0.35 → 0.5 gives +0.04%; lower values are untested.
- The observations carry no advance holiday information. Mechanism 1 detects a holiday from the absence of arrivals.

**Ceilings and dead ends already mapped**
- Per-zone occupancy is not inferable from observations well enough: precision 0.72–0.86 against the 0.93–0.98 needed. An oracle would be worth −2.3% (`docs/voi_oracle.md`).
- A differentiable twin's exact open-loop optimum is only ~0.16% better than the controller at matched comfort compliance (`docs/exact_opt.md`, `docs/opt_gap.md`, `docs/control_gap.md`).
- The cooling season has no night release. Seven mechanisms that do not close that gap are documented in `docs/cool_gap.md`.
- Margin losers vs winners: the losers' zones exceed the cooling setpoint on 0.3–0.8% of occupied zone-steps, against ~0.01% in the winners. Wu's `model_error` is *lower* in the losers, so the drift is systematic, not noise. Uniform margins cannot serve both groups; that is why #9 adapts per zone.

## Dead ends (measured; do not retry without a new angle)

- **Occupancy and forecasting:** OccInfer (per-zone occupancy inference); LP horizon; night flush; frost latch; forecast preheat; load shifting by hour.
- **Hot water:**
  - fixed tank targets (330 K: +0.24%; 345 K: split);
  - fast-rise water reset;
  - tank pre-charge after a setback (30-day fb1 −0.008%; the bottleneck is the air side and the mass);
  - removing Wu's 5% pump headroom (≤ 0.03% possible).
- **Frost-floor duty cycling** (2026-11-09; four designs, all on fb1 Saturday): per-zone latch to floor+2 K (+25% Saturday, min zone 3.9 °C: non-burst zones cooled by the ventilation), 7-step group bursts to floor+2 K with warm zones held (+45%: the air relaxes to the fabric within one off-step, retrigger every 8 steps), 18-step bursts to +1.5 K (+150%) and 36-step bursts to +1 K (+300%; the "hold warm zones where they are" rule kept the whole building at 13–16 °C all Saturday). The air cannot store heat and the fabric sits below the floor: the hold must be continuous.
- **Per-zone earned arrival lead** (lead_z ± 0.25/0.5 h from the first-30-min shortfall): worse than any uniform lead (denver_2 +0.9% vs −0.4%): once leads differ inside an AHU group, the plant runs for one zone while the others are ventilated at the minimum flow (or held, which costs the same).
- **Cooling-side late start** (standby ceiling until 0.5 h before comfort instead of Wu's 1 h ramp): singapore_g6 +2.3%, phoenix_4 +4.9%, singapore_4 +2.3%, atlanta_g4 +0.5%. The last pre-arrival hour of cooling pays (cooler outside air, cheaper electricity).
- **Late-start lead 0.75 h**: benchmark 0.008737483 (−0.05% vs 1 h) but comfort 0.50 → 0.76 and split (atlanta_8 −1.1%, denver_2 −0.3% vs fairbanks_g6 +0.7%); 1 h kept pending the paired 30-day run.
- **Model gain and tracking:**
  - gain 0.95;
  - oscillation-adaptive per-zone gain (ties fixed 0.80; a fallback);
  - `residual_alpha` 0 / 1;
  - `disturbance_rate` 0.5;
  - `recovery_steps` 1.0 / 1.25 / 1.75;
  - full cooling-side recovery (+7.2%);
  - per-zone cooling integral.
- **Plant:** AHU skip in comfort mode; AHU duty cycling at the 200 Pa / 0.1-damper floors; `gas_ratio_scale` 1.1.
- **Margins** (run #9 is the adaptive version):
  - uniform margin 0.06 / 0.04;
  - separate arrival margin;
  - ceiling 0.08 K, at either ramp rate;
  - separate lower/upper relaxation (identical to 9 digits);
  - reset on the first step outside the window;
  - 0.02 K anticipatory trigger;
  - settle 3 h instead of 1 h (ties).
- **Morning:**
  - arrival lead 0.75 h, and a setback-aware lead (+0.05/+0.18% on 30-day fb1);
  - night lift 3.0 K, or a uniform 1.0 K;
  - `preheat_safety` 0.75;
  - `preheat_max_hours` 4;

## Open directions (for phase 2 and the paper)

- **Charge scheduling with the identified model (the research line).** The identified zone model predicts a step 3–5× better than the base (table in the 2026-11-09 log), yet plugged into the one-step search it loses, because precise tracking of the setpoint during the lead buys comfort the reward does not pay for. The right use of the model is to *plan the morning charge*: choose, per group, the start time and air trajectory that minimises gas × the ventilation multiplier $1+0.3(T_z-T_o)/(T_{dis}-T_z)$ subject to the air reaching the setpoint at 06:00, with the fabric state estimated online. Optimum start (Seem et al.; Armstrong's thermal-response model; Cheng & Taylor, ASHRAE Journal 2024) is the classical frame; the twist is the ventilation floor, which makes early charging cheap per watt in deep cold and pure overhead in mild weeks. Mechanism 10 is the two-point version of that policy; the gate (4 K) and the lead (0.75 h) are what a model-based rule should replace.
- **Closed-loop identification hygiene.** The RLS was fit on the controller's own closed-loop data: the gain estimate is biased by feedback and the lagged-change regressor makes the one-step prediction chase its own last step. Instrumental variables (the previous action as instrument), excitation only from the free-float nights, or a two-node state estimator (air + fabric, Kalman) with the physical coupling 5 W/m²K as prior are the candidates. Grey-box RC identification (Bacher & Madsen 2011) is the reference.
- **Lead per building, reward-consistent — closed (2026-11-11).** With hindsight, the best of 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0 h per scenario is worth −0.021% on dev30; only building 5 wants a longer lead. The per-zone size scaling of v7 (mechanism 13) gets −0.064% without hindsight, including −0.5% on those two scenarios.
- **Climate adaptation from observations.** Margin, lift and lead trade differently by building; observed statistics (outside-air history, recovery speed, pump saturation) are allowed inputs. Mechanisms 7, 8 and 10 are instances.
